### Why get certified at all?

I get asked this a lot. Are Linux certifications worth it?

A certification is a way to prove to a recruiter that you have the skill. Anyone can list "Kubernetes" or "Linux administration" on a resume. A certification from an industry-recognized body like the Linux Foundation tells the hiring manager that someone has actually tested you on it.

This matters even more for performance-based exams like CKA, CKS and LFCS. You don't pick answers from a list. You fix real problems on a live system within a time limit, which is pretty much what the job looks like.

Certifications are even more helpful if you are a fresh graduate, or if you are switching to a new domain like cloud native or security.

And it is not just my opinion. In the [Linux Foundation’s 2026 State of Tech Talent report][17], 76% of organizations said they value certifications when assessing candidates for a technical role. That is slightly ahead of college degrees at 74%, and this is the third year in a row that certifications have ranked above degrees.

### You don't have to take the exam this week

This is the part a lot of people miss. You only need to buy the certification before October 23. You don't need to take the exam right away.

Linux Foundation certifications come with 12 months of exam eligibility. You can schedule and take the exam any time within a year of purchase. So you can lock in the discounted price now and prepare at your own pace.

Most certifications also include one free retake. If your first attempt does not go well, you get a second chance without paying again. That's a big relief, in my opinion.
